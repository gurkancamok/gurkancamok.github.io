The allocation problem

As research and quality-improvement requests increase, advisor allocation needs to consider expertise and capacity together. A useful system should explain why an advisor is recommended and preserve human review.

My technical contribution

I created the browser application, expertise-matching rules, capacity-aware ranking, explanation display and browser-local assignment trail.

  1. Exclude advisors who have reached their defined capacity.
  2. Calculate fit against the selected clinical and research domains.
  3. Calculate availability from current workload and capacity.
  4. Combine expertise fit and availability with an explicit 70% / 30% weighting.
  5. Rank by the composite score, using workload and stable advisor ID to resolve ties.
  6. Record the recommendation rationale for user review.

Technology: HTML, CSS and JavaScript. The public demonstration uses fictional advisor identities and browser-local storage.

Operational context and demonstration scope

The Research Advisor Matching system is deployed and in use at the hospital. The advisor-allocation workflow is reported to have supported approximately 100 allocations across 11 advisors. These figures describe the institutional workflow, not traffic or transactions in the public demonstration.

The demonstration illustrates the software design with fictional information. It is not connected to a hospital database and does not disclose internal advisor identities or research records.

Human review and limits

The selected weights make the logic inspectable; they are design choices, not evidence of an optimal allocation model. Recommendations support an allocation decision and do not replace the responsible team’s judgement.

Do not enter patient, employee or confidential research information into the demonstration. Data entered there remains in the visitor’s browser.
