Digital Insulin Infusion Assistant

A defined institutional insulin protocol, implemented as deterministic, reviewable software and evaluated against an independent reference standard.

The clinical problem

Applying an IV insulin protocol requires several linked decisions: selecting the current glucose band, normalising change over the measurement interval, finding the decision-table rule and mapping the existing infusion rate to an adjustment.

I developed the assistant to bring these steps into a structured browser workflow while retaining the original protocol, escalation instructions and clinician responsibility.

My technical contribution

I was responsible for requirements analysis, translation of the hospital protocol into software, interface development, structured input checks, testing, the validation study and implementation coordination.

The hospital owns the clinical protocol. My contribution is the executable implementation and its user-facing decision workflow.

  • Time-normalised glucose-trend calculation.
  • Clinical-state classification and protocol-rule selection.
  • Infusion-rate band matching, including Δ and 2Δ adjustments.
  • Hypoglycaemia pathways, monitoring intervals and physician-notification prompts.
  • Turkish and English outputs with source-protocol information retained in the interface.

Implementation: HTML, CSS and JavaScript. The clinical calculation is deterministic and browser-based, with no machine-learning prediction model.

From inputs to a reviewable action

  1. Structured clinical inputsCurrent and previous glucose, elapsed measurement interval and current infusion rate.
  2. Hourly glucose trendNormalise the glucose difference to the stated measurement interval.
  3. Protocol rule and rate bandSelect the applicable clinical pathway and adjustment band.
  4. Action, monitoring and escalationPresent a reviewable protocol output for the treating clinician.

The source rules remain available within the application. The software does not control an infusion pump or administer insulin.

Evaluation design and results

The prospective paired shadow evaluation included 120 different patients and 120 different nurses between August and October 2025. Manual calculations were used in care. I entered the same recorded inputs into the application the following day, so digital outputs did not influence treatment.

Two endocrinologists independently calculated the protocol-reference rates while blinded to each other’s work, the manual calculations and the digital outputs. They agreed in all 120 cases.

Institutional approval and implementation

The Medication Management and Use Committee and Medical Directorate reviewed and approved the application before routine deployment. The adoption notice identifies me as the developer.

In January 2026 it was made available across intensive care, emergency and inpatient settings at Anadolu Medical Center to approximately 400 healthcare professionals. This is an access figure, not a verified count of active users or calculation sessions.

The application does not store patient data. Institutional use depends on the approved protocol, clinical governance and clinician review.

Current scope and next evaluation

  • The evidence comes from a single institution and its protocol.
  • The 120-case result does not prove error-free performance in every possible case or later software revision.
  • The portfolio’s current public build is inspectable; the original validation evidence relates to the study build. Each material revision needs verification.
  • The system remains an internal, non-commercial clinical decision-support tool. No CE marking, MDR conformity assessment or NHS deployment is claimed.
  • External multicentre evaluation, independent-user usability work and clinical-outcome monitoring remain future development priorities.
